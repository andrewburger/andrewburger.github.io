There is a line from an article by Geoffrey Huntley’s [Nobody should type code by hand anymore. Here’s what’s left.](https://ghuntley.com/agency/) that has stuck with me:

> It was a mindset constriction, not a model constriction.
>
> ~ *Josh Wulf* 

Today I came across [Reverse Engineer Anything](https://github.com/morluto/rea) (REA), and it blew my mind. Then I found [ArtCraft](https://getartcraft.com/apps), an ambitious effort to build open-source alternatives to familiar creative and productivity software on [GitHub](https://github.com/storytold). And [a recent report from Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/we-might-be-cooked-as-these-vibe-coded-web-browser-ports-of-halo-the-simpsons-hit-and-run-and-gta-vice-city-seem-to-work-perfectly-2000743300) described AI-assisted projects bringing older video games into the browser with surprisingly impressive results.

Different projects, different motivations. Some are driven by frustration with subscription models and the desire for more control over the tools we use. Others seem motivated by curiosity, experimentation, and the sheer fun of asking, "What if?"

Ideas that once required specialized expertise and substantial engineering effort can increasingly be replicated, remixed, and reimagined in a fraction of the time.

The implications extend well beyond developer productivity or automating knowledge work. The boundaries of what's realistic and feasible are changing, opening up possibilities for motivated people willing to think beyond the established way of doing things.

As implementation through AI gets cheaper, the engineering bottleneck shifts toward judgment: knowing what to build, directing the work, and verifying that the result actually works. Huntley's article explores these implications for software engineering, but the consequences extend much further.

Software businesses, competitive advantages, intellectual property, and entire careers have been shaped by the cost and difficulty of building things. As these constraints fall, assumptions around them are less durable.

AI can certainly help us do the same things faster but the real opportunity is to rethink why and what we are building, what makes it valuable, and which constraints we've accepted simply because they have always been there.
